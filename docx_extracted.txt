Part A - Audit of the Existing System Concept
This part audits the earlier DAGACS/DAMAS proposals against the corrected requirements. The audit covers role definitions, attendance workflow, data model, permissions, security, scalability, reporting, and research positioning. Every finding is followed by the correction applied in Part B.
A.1 Headline Audit Findings
Contradiction in the attendance workflow (critical).
The earlier design combined a 24-hour submission window, a late-attendance request flow, an HOD approval workflow, an attendance lock after 48 hours, and late-attendance notifications. These features directly contradict the corrected requirement that a teacher may update attendance at any later time without HOD approval. All of these mechanisms are rejected and removed. The only correct mechanism for governing a later change is the automatic Audit Log.
Role model inconsistency.
Most proposal documents defined three roles (Admin, HOD, Teacher); only the full Scope of Work included Student. The corrected system defines exactly four roles, with Student as a read-only self-service role.
Teacher-created student records were wrongly allowed.
The earlier teacher module included 'Import Student List (if required)'. This leads to duplicate and inconsistent student masters. The corrected design keeps a single centralized student master maintained by the Admin (with Excel/CSV import); teachers only view students already assigned to their class.
Compliance as a concept was undefined.
The earlier 'Compliance Monitoring Engine' measured on-time submission against the 24-hour rule. With that rule removed, the term 'Compliance' had no meaning. In the corrected system, Compliance is redefined as three legitimate, low-complexity capabilities (see section B.1).
Missing HOD department binding and data isolation.
The design never enforced that an HOD belongs to exactly one department or that HOD, Teacher, and Student queries are scoped to their own data. The corrected system makes department/section/student scoping an explicit architectural and security requirement.
Database weaknesses.
No uniqueness constraints on teacher-subject-section assignment, no snapshot data in the audit log (roll number, student name, subject, section), ambiguous password storage, and a LEAVE status that the corrected requirements do not define.
Research positioning relied on the rejected late-approval workflow.
The claimed novelty was dominated by late-approval and submission-compliance features. With those removed, the research contributions are repositioned around the audit subsystem, role-based data isolation, governance workflows, and department analytics (see section B.13).

B.1 System Name and Meaning of Compliance
The title 'Department Attendance Governance, Analytics and Compliance System (DAGACS)' is accurate provided the term Compliance is explicitly scoped. With the late-approval mechanisms removed, Compliance is defined as the following three legitimate capabilities, none of which adds an approval workflow:
These three capabilities give the word 'Compliance' a real, verifiable meaning without reintroducing any rejected feature.
B.2 Corrected System Architecture
The system remains a three-tier architecture. This is appropriate for the problem.
Functional modules:
Authentication & Authorization (JWT + RBAC + row-level scoping)
Master Data Management (Admin): department, academic year, semester, section, subject, teacher, student, user
Teaching Assignment: teacher -> subject -> section -> session
Attendance Management: mark, view, update (update always creates audit entry)
Audit Subsystem: immutable append-only change log
Student Self-Service: view own attendance, percentages, history
HOD Governance: department dashboard, analytics, low-attendance, coverage, audit view
Reporting: Excel (required) and PDF (justified optional)
Notification: minimal in-app + email for low-attendance alerts

B.4 Complete Functional Workflows
B.4.1 Academic setup workflow (Admin)
Step 1: Admin creates the department and marks one teacher as its HOD.
Step 2: Admin creates the academic session (academic year).
Step 3: Admin creates semesters under the session.
Step 4: Admin creates sections under each semester (department-scoped).
Step 5: Admin creates subjects and links them to semesters.
Step 6: Admin registers teachers and creates their login accounts.
Step 7: Admin imports students from Excel/CSV into the centralized master and assigns them to sections.
Step 8: Admin creates teaching assignments: teacher -> subject -> section -> session.
B.4.2 Attendance marking workflow (Teacher)
Step 1: Teacher logs in; the system loads only the teacher's teaching assignments.
Step 2: Teacher selects assigned subject and section.
Step 3: System displays the student list for that section.
Step 4: Teacher marks each student Present or Absent.
Step 5: Teacher submits attendance; records are stored with the date and teacher identity.
Step 6: If attendance already exists for that student/subject/date, the system returns a clear 'already recorded' message.
B.4.3 Attendance update workflow (Teacher, auto-audit)
Step 1: Teacher opens a previously recorded attendance for an assigned class.
Step 2: Teacher changes a status, e.g. Absent -> Present.
Step 3: The system updates the attendance record.
Step 4: The Audit subsystem automatically inserts an audit entry capturing old value, new value, student, roll number, subject, section, attendance date, changed-by, date/time, and optional reason.
Step 5: No approval workflow is triggered.
B.4.4 Student self-service workflow (Student, read-only)
Step 1: Student logs in.
Step 2: System loads only the student's own records from their section.
Step 3: Student views subject-wise attendance, overall percentage, monthly, semester, and history.
Step 4: Student receives low-attendance alerts when below the threshold.
Step 5: No write access to any attendance record.
B.4.5 HOD governance workflow (HOD, department-scoped)
Step 1: HOD logs in; the system loads only their department.
Step 2: HOD views the dashboard: overall attendance %, section comparison, subject comparison, low-attendance list, coverage gaps, recent audit activity.
Step 3: HOD drills into section-wise, subject-wise, and student-wise attendance.
Step 4: HOD reviews the Attendance Audit Log.
Step 5: HOD exports monthly, quarterly, semester, or low-attendance reports to Excel (or PDF).
B.4.6 Attendance percentage rule (single source of truth)
Percentage = (number of PRESENT records / total recorded records) x 100, computed only from recorded attendance rows. Days with no record are not counted because the system has no timetable. The same formula is used by Student view, HOD analytics, and reports so that every view is consistent.
B.5 Database Entities and Relationships
Important relationships and constraints:
teacher.dept_id -> department.id; HOD is a teacher with is_hod = true in exactly one department.
section belongs to one department and one semester.
student.section_id -> section.id; a student is never duplicated (unique roll_no).
teaching_assignment prevents duplicate teacher-subject-section-session rows.
attendance.unique prevents duplicate attendance for the same student in the same class on the same date.
audit_log is append-only; no update or delete endpoint exists.
Indexes: attendance(teaching_assignment_id, attendance_date), attendance(student_id), audit_log(attendance_id), audit_log(changed_by).
Password storage: BCrypt hash; never plain text, never reversible encryption.

B.6 API / Module Implementation Plan
Base path /api/v1. Response envelope: { success, message, data, timestamp }. JWT in Authorization: Bearer <token>. Pagination on list endpoints.
B.7 Realistic Implementation Roadmap
Agile development with 2-week sprints. The roadmap is simpler than the earlier plan because the late-approval workflow and second frontend were removed.
Total functional scope is deliberately contained: one frontend, no approval workflows, no real-time infrastructure, no external hardware. This is realistic for an M.Tech timeline while remaining architecturally complete.
B.8 Functional and Non-Functional Requirements
Functional requirements (by module):
Authentication: login, logout, change password, role-based redirection; accounts created by Admin.
Master data: CRUD for department, session, semester, section, subject, teacher, student; deactivate/activate accounts.
Student import: Excel/CSV upload; validation; duplicate detection by roll number; import summary report.
Teaching assignment: create/remove teacher-subject-section-session; enforce uniqueness; list assignments per teacher.
Attendance: mark Present/Absent for a class on a date; block duplicate per student/class/date; update status later.
Audit: automatic immutable log on every update with old/new value, student, roll no, subject, section, date, user, timestamp, optional reason.
Student self-service: own subject-wise %, overall %, monthly, semester, history; no write access.
HOD governance: department dashboard; section/subject/student analytics; low-attendance list; coverage view; audit log view.
Reports: daily-lecture, monthly, quarterly, semester, low-attendance, coverage; Excel export required, PDF justified.
Notifications: in-app + email for low-attendance alerts; mark-as-read.
Non-functional requirements:
Security: BCrypt password hashing; JWT; method-level RBAC; row-level scoping in the service layer; DTOs; input validation; append-only audit.
Data isolation: teacher, student, and HOD can never read or write outside their scope, even with forged identifiers.
Performance: department-scale (a few thousand students, < 100 teachers); list endpoints paginated; response time < 2s for dashboard/report generation.
Auditability: every attendance change traceable; audit records cannot be modified or deleted by any role.
Reliability: ACID transactions for attendance + audit write; consistent percentage computation.
Usability: simple role-specific UI; < 3 clicks to mark a class; clear import error messages.
Maintainability: layered Spring Boot services, Swagger/OpenAPI, documented code, 80%+ test coverage target.
Scalability (future-ready): normalized schema and indexes allow multi-department extension without redesign.
B.9 Scope Separation
Current scope (this M.Tech deliverable):
Four roles with strict separation (Admin, HOD, Teacher, Student).
Centralized academic master data with Excel/CSV student import.
Attendance marking, viewing, and unrestricted later correction by the teacher.
Automatic, immutable attendance audit log.
Student read-only self-service and low-attendance alerts.
HOD department-scoped dashboard, analytics, coverage, and audit viewing.
Excel and PDF report export.
Future scope (genuinely relevant, not artificial):
Separate Flutter mobile app for Teacher/Student (responsive web covers current needs).
Timetable/period scheduling, enabling precise 'classes not recorded' detection and per-lecture attendance.
Real-time dashboard refresh via WebSocket.
Push notifications via FCM.
Multiple departments and a super-admin across departments.
Leave/holiday calendar and LEAVE status if the institution requires it.
PDF report branding per institution.
Out of scope (explicitly rejected for this project):
24-hour submission deadline and late-attendance workflow.
HOD approval for normal attendance updates.
Late-attendance notifications.
Face recognition, GPS/geofencing, QR attendance, RFID, biometrics.
Parent module.
Student editing of attendance.
AI/ML attendance prediction.
Generic ERP features beyond the defined scope (payroll, grading, library, finance, etc.).
B.10 Research Positioning and Evaluation (M.Tech)
Research contributions (honest, no artificial AI):
Attendance Governance Framework (AGF): a role-governed lifecycle for attendance data (record, correct, audit, analyze).
An immutable, self-contained audit subsystem design for attendance changes, including denormalized snapshots that survive master-data edits.
Multi-level data isolation (RBAC + row-level scoping) applied to academic data, with explicit security evaluation.
A department analytics model: consistent percentage computation, threshold/eligibility monitoring, and coverage analytics.
An automated reporting pipeline (Excel/PDF) driven by the same query layer used by dashboards.
Evaluation plan:
Correctness: compare computed percentages and low-attendance lists against manual calculation on a seeded dataset.
Audit completeness: verify that 100% of programmatically performed updates produce correct audit entries.
Data isolation: automated tests confirming cross-role access attempts are rejected (teacher/student/HOD).
Usability: task-based evaluation with real teachers and HOD (e.g., System Usability Scale).
Performance: attendance marking time, report generation time, and dashboard load time on department-scale data.
Comparison: manual register vs DAGACS on time-to-record and time-to-report.
